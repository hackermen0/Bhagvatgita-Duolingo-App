# Handover

Mobile-first, Duolingo-style Bhagavad Gita learning app. SvelteKit 5 (runes) + Tailwind v4, deployed on Vercel (`adapter-auto`, `engines.node: 20.x`). No backend: all progress is in `localStorage` (`gita_game_state`). Git HEAD at time of writing: `317ba45`, working tree clean.

Run: `npm run dev` · type-check: `npm run check` (0 errors / 0 warnings at last run).

---

## 1. How the learning flow works now

Each verse is **one node** on the path (no rings) that plays a fixed **19-page journey**, defined by `JOURNEY_PAGES` in `src/lib/data/journey.ts` and played by `src/lib/components/JourneyScreen.svelte` (route `/lesson/[lessonId]`, no `?level=`).

| Pages | What the learner does |
|---|---|
| 1 | Intro: verse in Hindi (tap a word to hear it) and English, plus the deeper meaning, each with a voice |
| 2–7 | Per half of the verse: **word flashcards** (`WordCard`, flip cards with Hindi/Slow/English voices) → **word matching** on those same 4 words → **phrase matching** |
| 8–14 | Fill in the blanks: words per half → phrases per half → one phrase per half → the whole verse (`BlankFill`) |
| 15–17 | Audio recital: Hindi or English, per half then the whole verse (`ReciteScreen`) |
| 18 | Re-exam: the words missed on earlier pages, with a clue (the phrase each came from). With no mistakes, a spot-check on words the word pages never showed |
| 19 | Reflection (`ReflectionPrompt`), then the completion flow (`CompleteFlow`: stats → unit story → streak) |

- **Content** is `JourneyContent` (`journey.ts`). BG 2.48 is hand-authored in `journeyContent.ts` (from the revamped beginner doc); every other verse is generated by `generateJourney` from its `parts` and `wordBreakdown`. Add an entry to `JOURNEY_OVERRIDES` to hand-author any verse. A half with a single part has its English split in two so the chunk pages differ.
- **Word rotation:** each half has ~12 words, but its flashcards and word-matching page work on the same 4 or 5 (`pickWordBatch`, `batchSizeFor`): a seeded *weighted random* draw from the half's content words (noun ×3, divided down as the learner's memory strength rises, so a replay leans towards unmet words; whether a word has a picture doesn't matter); grammar words only pad a half with too few content words. Each half only draws from words said in that half's own Hindi (`containsPhrase`, enforced in `JourneyScreen`; a word in both halves, like tatha or mein, belongs to both). A word with no picture shows the Krishna mascot with "Default picture will be updated" under it (`WordFlashcard`). Every fresh start (new seed, incl. Start over and replays) draws again; a resume rebuilds the saved batch. Measured on BG 2.48: ~45 distinct sets per half over 200 seeds, ~72% nouns, no grammar words. A half with fewer than 4–5 content words (e.g. BG 2.47 half 2 has 4) can't vary much without grammar words. Practice (`lessonWords` → `journeyWordsFor`) and the re-exam spot-check use all of them, so grammar words get tested there. Only words actually shown/asked are credited in `wordMemory` on completion; the rest stay due.
- **Checkpoints:** `gameState.journeyCheckpoint[lessonId]` = `{page, seed, batches, missedWords, seenWords, correct, wrong, elapsedMs}`, saved after every finished page, with the journey's page count (a checkpoint from a different layout is discarded on load). The seed fixes the word batches and decoys, so resuming rebuilds the same pages. The path node shows `N/19` and its popover offers Continue / Start over. Running out of hearts refills them and replays the current page; earlier pages stay saved.
- **Completion:** `gameState.completeVerse` (50 XP first time, 15 on replay) marks the verse done and clears the checkpoint. The old `levelProgress` / `completeLevel` / 3-level design is gone; saves that had `levelProgress` simply lose any half-finished level.
- **Tiers:** all tiers play the same 19 pages; the tier picks the language and script (see section 1a: Hindi/Roman, Sanskrit/Roman, Sanskrit/Devanagari; `scriptText` picks the script). (A separate 17/19-page Beginner layout for BG 2.48 from the beginner doc was built and then removed: the owner wanted the original order, flashcards → word matching → phrase matching, with word matching on the flashcards' 4 words, not all 12. What stayed from that doc: BG 2.48's "(Equanimity)" phrase wording, `tatha` = "as well as / and", and tappable English words on the intro. `yog` stays "yoga" with the "means equanimity here" note, where the doc says "Equanimity" on half 1's word page.)
- **Word highlighting:** `SpokenText.svelte` shows a verse/phrase as words and lights each one as it is spoken (Hindi or English); used on the intro, the recital pages (plays itself when its text is visible) and the Hindi card on the matching/blank pages. It runs on `speakTokens` in `speech.ts`: one utterance with `onboundary` on desktop, one utterance per word on phones whose voices never report boundaries (detected once and cached in `localStorage`, key `gita_tts_word_boundaries_v2[_en]`). Verified against a stubbed speech engine in both modes, not with a real voice.
- **Recital waveform:** `VoiceWave.svelte` (25 bars) in `ReciteScreen` shows the mic state: still dots (off), gentle breathing (open, waiting), green dancing (voice detected), with a matching label ("Starting the microphone…" / "Listening…" / "I can hear you…"). It is driven by the recognition engine's own events (`onaudiostart`, `onspeechstart/end`, partial results; `listen()` takes `onReady` / `onSpeaking`) — **not** by microphone loudness, so the bars dance in a fixed pattern rather than following volume. A true level meter would need a second `getUserMedia` stream, which some phones can't share with `SpeechRecognition`. Verified against a stubbed recognition engine, not a real microphone.
- **Voice:** `src/lib/utils/speech.ts` (`speak(text, 'hi' | 'en')`, `listen`, `hasRecognition`) and `SpeakButton`. Recital uses `SpeechRecognition` where the browser has it (Chromium) and a typing box otherwise; grading (`scoreRecital`) is lenient (60% of words) and never costs hearts.
- **Dev menu:** Settings → "Developer menu · for testing" (collapsed; visible in all builds for now — gate it on `import.meta.env.DEV` before release). Complete / Reset any single verse, complete or reset all, refill hearts, forget learned words. Backed by `gameState.devSetLessonsComplete` and `devClearWordMemory`; no XP/streak changes.
- `QuizScreen.svelte` is now only the engine for **practice** and **jump tests** (one flat queue; `mode` is required).

## 1a. The three tiers: Hindi in Roman, Sanskrit in Roman, Sanskrit in Devanagari

- **Beginner** = Hindi in Roman letters (Hinglish). **Medium** = the original Sanskrit in Roman letters (IAST, as in the Medium doc). **Hard** = the Sanskrit in Devanagari. `scriptModeForTier` (gameState): Hard → `devanagari`, Beginner and Medium → `roman_hindi` (Roman). The learner always answers in English. The copy in onboarding (`DIFFICULTY_TIERS`) and Settings follows this.
- **Only BG 2.48 has Sanskrit content so far.** Every other verse is still Hindi at every tier, so at Medium it reads like Beginner and at Hard it is Hindi in Devanagari, until their Sanskrit content is authored. The onboarding/Settings copy promises Sanskrit for every verse.
- `getJourneyContent(lesson, seed, tier)` looks in `TIER_JOURNEY_OVERRIDES[tier]` (`journeyContent.ts`) before the all-tier `JOURNEY_OVERRIDES` and the generated journey. `bg248Sanskrit` (from the Medium doc) is registered for both `medium` and `hard`, so they play the same content in the tier's script; it uses the same 19 pages and the same word picking as every verse. To add Sanskrit for another verse, author a `JourneyContent` like it and register it for `medium` and `hard`.
- Fields named `hindi` hold the Sanskrit. Medium shows `roman` (the doc's IAST), Hard shows `dev`; the voice always speaks `dev`, with the device's Hindi (`hi-IN`) voice since there is no Sanskrit voice. `foldRoman` (in `hindi.ts`) strips diacritics inside `wordKey` and `plain`, so IAST works for keys, matching and typing (`karmāṇi` → `karmani`).
- New optional `JourneyContent` fields: `language: 'sanskrit'` (labels say Sanskrit instead of Hindi: intro, flashcards, matching, recital), `variant`, `blankClue: 'english'` (blank pages are in Sanskrit with the English half as the clue, as in the doc), `recitalClues: ['text','opposite-audio','audio']` (page 14 hides the text and speaks the *other* language as the clue: choose Sanskrit → you hear the English; "Show hint" reveals the text). `BlankSet.answerKeys` lists the word keys behind each answer, since the heuristic that maps an English answer to words can't read Sanskrit. `BlankSet.dev` is the Devanagari twin of a Roman blank set (same option order); `blankInScript` picks it for Hard, and `BlankFill`'s `spoken` prop makes a Roman option speak from its Devanagari.
- The Hard doc differs from the Medium one only in the explanation (page 1 names the key words in Devanagari) and the yoga-sthaḥ note (page 3): `JourneyContent.deeperMeaningDev` and `JourneyWord.noteDev` hold those, and `JourneyIntro` / `WordFlashcard` use them when the tier reads Devanagari. The Medium explanation names them in Roman (`saṅgaṁ`, `siddhy-asiddhyoḥ`, `samatvaṁ`) rather than the Hindi `aasakti` / `samabhav` the Medium doc kept. The Hard doc's later pages (words, phrases, blanks, recitals) were still in Roman, so Hard shows Devanagari throughout, as the tier says. The English voice reads the Devanagari terms inside the explanation poorly (it has no Hindi voice for them).
- Doc fixes: its "siddhyi" is read as `siddhy`; the page-3 answer key's duplicate "A)" is ignored. The blanks keep the doc's decoys, which include the other half's words. `kuru`, `karmāṇi` and `tyaktvā` reuse the existing `perform`, `duty` and `abandon` pictures.
- A checkpoint carries `variant` (`journeyVariantFor`: the content's `variant`, or the tier's name, when the tier has its own content for the verse; else `'base'`). `gameState.checkpointFor` hides one saved under another variant, so Medium and Hard share progress on BG 2.48 (both `'sanskrit'`) while Beginner's `'base'` is separate. Only one checkpoint exists per verse, so playing it under the other variant overwrites it.
- Practice still uses the Hindi words (`journeyWordsFor` ignores tier), so Sanskrit words aren't practised; their word-memory entries are inert. Practice's script follows the tier (Roman on Beginner/Medium, Devanagari on Hard).
- Verified in headless Chrome (stubbed speech): the full Medium (Roman) and Hard (Devanagari) playthroughs, including a wrong blank → re-exam on exactly those words, the recital choices and clues, typing IAST, Roman blank chips spoken from Devanagari, Medium/Hard sharing a checkpoint and Beginner not seeing it, and the Beginner flow unchanged. Not verified: real voices or a real microphone on Sanskrit.

## 1b. Two learning modes

The learner picks a mode on the onboarding step after the welcome (`chooseMode`), and can switch in Settings → Learning mode. Stored as `gameState.learningMode` (`'normal' | 'philosophy'`, also on `profile.learningMode`).

- **Normal** is the 19-page journey above, unchanged.
- **Philosophy has its own path** (`pathChaptersFor(mode)` in `philosophy.ts`): **Chapter 1** ("Arjuna Vishada Yoga", unit "The Armies Gather") first, then the same Chapter 2 verses the normal mode teaches. Chapter 1 is philosophy-only: its nodes are minimal `Lesson` records (`stubLesson`) so the existing path, unlocks and dev menu work unchanged; it has no guidebook and no unit-review trophy (`isPhilosophyOnlySection`). `findLesson` looks up a node on either path; the normal mode never sees Chapter 1 ids ("Lesson not found").
- **Chapter 1 content** is `philosophyChapter1.ts`, transcribed from the philosophy doc: `PRELUDE_PAGES` (the opening "A Question From the Dark", the seven family chapters and "Start of the Gita") and `CHAPTER_1_VERSES` (BG 1.1–1.5: Sanskrit, English, "The philosophy" sections, 5 questions each). Added beyond the doc: each verse's Devanagari (spoken, and shown on Hard), and the title of 1.1 ("The Blind King's Question"). Inline `**bold**` / `*italic*` is rendered by `InlineText.svelte`.
- **The story before the Gita** is the first node (`PRELUDE_ID`, book icon) and `PreludeStory.svelte`: 9 full-screen pages like Instagram stories (progress bars, tap right = next, tap left = back, Skip, keyboard arrows). "Listen" turns on a voice-over that reads the page sentence by sentence (lighting the paragraph being read; Sanskrit lines spoken from Devanagari; `(Pause.)` / `(Final beat.)` from the doc are pauses) and moves on by itself, stopping on the last page. "Begin the Gita" completes it like a verse (30 XP) and unlocks 1.1. No images yet (the doc says "maybe ask Simran"); each page has its own dark backdrop.
- **A verse session** (`PhilosophyScreen.svelte`, built from `philosophySession(id)`, which normalises both chapters): the backstory reel when the verse has one (Chapter 2's narrated scenes; Chapter 1 has none, so it starts on the verse) → the verse and its meaning (Chapter 1: the Sanskrit in the tier's script + English + "The philosophy" sections, each with a Listen button; Chapter 2: the Hindi + English + "What it means" + commentary) → the quiz.
- **The questions are the app's regular multiple choice** (`MultipleChoice` + Check + `FeedbackModal` with the doc's explanation), no timer. A wrong answer costs a heart and comes back once at the end; running out of hearts offers "Refill hearts and retry" on the current question. Chapter 1 keeps the doc's option order; Chapter 2's options are shuffled (`questionOptions`). Accuracy = right on the first try. (A KBC-style timed quiz was tried and removed at the user's request.)
- **Content (Chapter 2)** is still `PHILOSOPHY` in `philosophy.ts`: backstory scenes, optional `video`, 4 questions per verse. **Chapter 2 backstories and questions were written by Claude and have not been reviewed.** A verse without content shows "Coming soon" in philosophy mode.
- **Videos: none exist yet.** Set `video: { kind: 'file', src: '/videos/….mp4' }` (file in `static/videos/`) or `{ kind: 'youtube', id }` on a verse to play it (Chapter 1 verses take a `backstory` with `video`).
- **Progress is per mode:** philosophy completions are `gameState.philosophyCompleted`; the path, unlocks, unit story and dev menu follow `gameState.isVerseDone` / `completedInMode`. Profile, top bar and guidebook count `versesLearned` (either mode). In philosophy mode the path has no jump tests (they test words; `jump` access falls back to sequential) and no "review first" advice. Practice/review is still word-based and only fills from normal-mode verses.
- Verified in headless Chrome (stubbed speech): onboarding into philosophy mode, BG 2.47 end to end (wrong answer → heart lost → retry at end → completion), switching back to normal mode in Settings (separate progress, journey opens). Not verified: a real video file/YouTube embed, real TTS voices.
- Chapter 1 verified in headless Chrome (stubbed speech): the path (Chapter 1 first, no guidebook/trophy there), the story (tap forward/back, voice-over moving on by itself, Skip, Sanskrit spoken from Devanagari, completion unlocking 1.1), BG 1.1 verse page and its questions (one wrong → heart lost → retried at the end → 80%), Hard showing Devanagari, a Chapter 2 verse (reel → Hindi verse → 4 questions), and the normal mode unchanged. Not verified: real voices, long narration on a phone.

## 2. Content (src/lib/data/gitaData.ts)

8 verses in Chapter 2 (BG 2.47, 2.48, 2.50, 2.71 in Unit 1; 2.13, 2.20, 2.22, 2.23 in Unit 2), 31 parts total.

- Every part has: matching (≥2 pairs) → `translate` → `sentence_rebuilding`, some end with a comprehension question. IDs: `…_m` (matching), `…_t` (translate), `…_r` (rebuild).
- `essence` (one-line meaning, shown on the verse intro), `verseWordGuide` (Devanagari↔roman token alignment), `hint` on single-phrase rebuilds (the part's English meaning).
- **All English glosses, explanations and `essence` lines were written by Claude and have not been reviewed by a Sanskrit-literate person.** Review before release.
- Every word shown in tiles/pairs/options must exist in `SANSKRIT_DICT` (`sanskritHelper.ts`) or it renders as raw IAST. A validation script (scratchpad, not in repo) checked: tiles spell targets, all words in the dictionary, all words taught earlier. Re-run an equivalent when adding content.

### The `translate` exercise ("Write this in English")
Graded by **meaning words, not order**: correct if the selection contains exactly the reference answer's non-grammar words and no decoys (grammar words like *is/in/the/of/it* are optional). A different phrasing shows "Another way to say it". Logic is in `QuizScreen.checkAnswer`, grammar-word set `GRAMMAR_WORDS`. Side effect: scrambled-but-complete word sets also pass (deliberate).

### WordTilePicker
Placed tiles can be **dragged to reorder** (pointer events, FLIP animation; tap still removes; arrow keys move a focused tile). `plain` prop renders English tiles. Tested with mouse, touch and keyboard on a throwaway page only.

## 4. Mascot animation (wired into the app)

**Now live in the app.** `Mascot.svelte` (used in ~20 places) renders the layered SVG and plays a reaction when its `mood` prop changes. The animation code is `src/lib/mascot/krishna.ts` (ported from `preview.template.html`, per-instance, with cleanup); the SVG it animates is `src/lib/mascot/krishna-animated.svg`, **generated** by `node design/mascot-svg/build-preview.cjs` alongside `preview.html` — re-run it after re-exporting any expression SVG. The SVG is a separate lazy chunk (`loadKrishnaSvg`, also started by `preloadMascots`).

- Mood → reaction: `default` idle/rest · `cheerful`, `proud` → correct (laughing + sparkles) · `excited`, `amazed` → combo · `celebrating` → celebrate (confetti) · `affectionate` → love · `thinking` → think · `disappointed` → wrong · `crying` → heartbreak. The `angry` reaction exists but no mood uses it.
- A reaction **holds its face** after the motion (the learner reads feedback with the mascot still smiling/sad); moving to another mood or `default` returns to the resting face.
- Moods with no animation (`shocked`, `worried`, `determined`, `mischievous`, `puppy`) show their static WebP over the (still mounted) SVG, then the animated mascot comes back. `prefers-reduced-motion` uses the WebPs only. Shocked/worried change the whole pose, so they are the next candidates for animation.
- Verified in headless Chrome (idle loops, laughing/disappointed/crying faces, confetti, shocked fallback, reduced motion, no console errors); not checked on a real phone for performance.
